Hasten Down the Wind Review by Amy Hanson

This aching, haunting ballad of the end of a love affair was one of Warren Zevon’s proudest moments, the world’s introduction to Zevon as one of the era’s most astute songwriters. The key to the song is not who dumped who; rather, it portrays unyielding heartache of a man bewildered by his lover’s need to stretch out toward independence, and his inability to understand why what they had was not enough. This is not a song of anger, of mistakes and resentment, but merely a moment in time where things are simply dropping away, where freedom becomes the weapon, and open eyes the hand that fires it. The timbre of ”Hasten Down The Wind” becomes even richer with Phil Everly’s spectral harmony. Linda Ronstadt took the song for the title track to her own simultaneously released album in August, 1976.
